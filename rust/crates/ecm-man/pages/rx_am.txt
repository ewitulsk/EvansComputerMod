NAME
    rx_am - listen to AM radio through a Speaker

SYNOPSIS
    rx_am <freq> [--squelch DB] [--gain DB] [--sdr NAME] [--rate SPS]
    [--speaker SIDE] [--volume 0-100] [--seconds S]
    [--wav FILE [--no-speaker]] [--audio-rate HZ]

DESCRIPTION
    Tunes the SDR next to the computer to freq, demodulates AM
    (broadcast stations, shortwave, aviation) and plays the audio on a
    Speaker touching the computer. The audio band is 4.5 kHz.

    It prints one line saying what it receives, then every 2 seconds the
    signal level in dBFS, and runs until Ctrl+T (or --seconds). With
    --seconds it ends with a summary of the strongest audio tone heard.

OPTIONS
    --squelch DB
        Mute the audio while the signal is below DB (dBFS). Off by
        default.

    --gain DB
        Fixed receiver gain in dB. Default: automatic gain (AGC).

    --sdr NAME
        Which SDR: empty (the first), 0 / sdr0 / sdr_0, a side of the
        computer (left, top, ...) or a /dev/sdr path.

    --rate SPS
        Sample rate. Default 48000.

    --speaker SIDE, --volume 0-100
        Which Speaker to use and its volume.

    --seconds S
        Stop after S seconds of samples.

    --wav FILE
        Also write the audio to a WAV file. With --no-speaker, only to
        the file.

    --audio-rate HZ
        Audio sample rate, 8000-48000. Default 24000.

EXAMPLES
      rx_am 11.6M
      rx_am 1.2M --volume 80
      rx_am 7.1M --seconds 20 --wav /am.wav --no-speaker

NOTES
    The Basic SDR tunes 0.5-1700 MHz, the Standard 10 kHz-6 GHz and
    the Advanced 1 kHz-6 GHz. For HF (3-30 MHz) a real antenna on coax
    hears far more than the SDR's built-in whip.
