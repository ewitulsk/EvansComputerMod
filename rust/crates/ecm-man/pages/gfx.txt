NAME
    gfx - how programs draw on a display, and timed frames for video

SYNOPSIS
    gfx_init2(target, w, h, format, flags)
    gfx_blit_rect(target, x, y, w, h, pixels, len, format)
    gfx_present(target, flags)
    gfx_present_at(target, flags, pts_us)
    gfx_queue_flush(target)
    gfx_set_hint(target, hint)
    gfx_queue_state(target)

DESCRIPTION
    This page is for people who write programs (in Rust, against the
    host ABI: ecm_host_abi::gfx_child). Players who just want to watch
    a video use videoplayer, video play or player.

    A program draws on a display: target 0 is the Terminal's own
    screen, target 1 the Screen wall attached to the computer.
    gfx_init2 takes the display over and sets its size, its pixel
    format (0 indexed8 with a palette, 1 rgba8888, 2 rgb565) and flags
    (1 = double-buffered). Other programs can't use it until this one
    exits or sets mode 0 (gfx_set_mode). The display refreshes at
    gfx_set_refresh Hz, at most what the panel and the server allow.

    gfx_blit_rect writes pixels. On a single-buffered display they show
    at once. On a double-buffered one they go into the back buffer, and
    gfx_present shows it (flags 1 = wait for the next vertical blank).

TIMED FRAMES
    A double-buffered display also has a presentation queue. Instead of
    showing the back buffer now, gfx_present_at queues it to be shown
    at pts_us: a time in microseconds on the program's monotonic clock,
    the clock a program reads with the WASI call clock_time_get
    (MONOTONIC), ecm_host_abi::gfx_child::monotonic_us in Rust. A video
    player decodes a little ahead and queues each frame for its own
    time; the server shows it then, and the players' games get the
    frames early and show them on time too, so the picture stays smooth
    when the server or the network hitch.

    A time that has passed, with nothing queued, shows the frame at
    once. A plain gfx_present with frames queued is shown after them.
    The queue holds at most 12 frames and 500 ms ahead (server config
    display.queueFrames and display.queueMs). When it is full,
    gfx_present_at fails with -11, or with flags 1 sleeps until there
    is room (that is how a player paces itself). A single-buffered
    display can't queue: -22.

    gfx_queue_flush drops every queued frame not shown yet (seek, pause,
    stop) and returns how many. gfx_queue_state returns frames queued
    in the high 32 bits and how many ms the last one is ahead of now in
    the low 32 bits (-1 for an unknown display).

    video_decode_to_back decodes the next frame of a video straight
    into the back buffer of a double-buffered display (made with
    gfx_init2 at the decoder's size and format) without showing it;
    gfx_present_at then shows it at its time.

LOSSY FRAMES
    gfx_set_hint says how the display's frames may travel to players:
    0 auto (the default: exact, lossy while most of the picture keeps
    changing), 1 exact (never lossy: text, pixel art), 2 motion (video:
    lossy allowed). Text on the console is always exact.

ERRORS
    -1 invalid argument, -2 another program owns the display, -3 this
    program doesn't own it, -4 no such display, -5 interrupted (the
    program is being stopped), -11 queue full, -22 single-buffered.

EXAMPLES
      init2(Target::Terminal, 320, 200, FORMAT_RGB565, FLAG_DOUBLE_BUFFER)
      let t0 = monotonic_us() + 100_000;
      // for each frame: draw, then
      present_at(Target::Terminal, PRESENT_AT_WAIT, t0 + pts_ms * 1000)
