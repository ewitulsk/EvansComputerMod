NAME
    voice - microphones, your voice, and talking over the radio

DESCRIPTION
    Computers hear sound the way real ones do: a microphone is a capture
    device programs read from, and a radio carries a voice as a real
    signal. A voice that reaches a computer has travelled through the
    air (fainter with distance) or over the air (with range, walls and
    other radios).

HARDWARE
    Microphone
        A desk microphone block. Next to a Terminal it is a microphone
        peripheral and /dev/mic; its tally lamp lights while a program
        records.

    ePhone
        Every ePhone has a microphone at its holder's hand, or uses its
        AirPods' (see bluetooth).

    Walkie-Talkie
        A two-way FM radio for VHF and UHF channels. While its holder
        holds its push-to-talk key it transmits their voice; any
        receiver on the channel hears it (another Walkie-Talkie, a
        Handheld Radio, an SDR running rx_fm).

    Speaker
        Plays sound, and is a source microphones hear.

THE SOUND IN THE AIR
    Every sound (a talking player, a Speaker, a phone's speaker) is put
    into the world at its position. A microphone hears all of them,
    each half as loud at twice the distance, and nothing past 24 blocks.
    A program that plays what it records next to its own Microphone
    feeds back, as in a real room.

/DEV/MIC
    /dev/mic (or /dev/mic.<side>) reads PCM; /dev/micctl sets the format
    (rate 8000-48000, bits 8|16, channels 1|2), the gain (0-100, 50 is
    the capsule's own level) and flush, and reads back the status and
    the level. Every open /dev/mic is its own stream, so two programs
    can record at once. Apps need the microphone permission.

YOUR MICROPHONE
    Minecraft's Options > Music & Sounds has Microphone Input (off by
    default), the input device, its volume and the mode: Push to Talk
    (hold Talk, B) or Voice Activation. The microphone opens only while
    something wants your voice (a Microphone near you that a program
    reads, or a Walkie-Talkie in hand), and the HUD shows MIC or TX.
    With Simple Voice Chat or Plasmo Voice installed, that mod's
    microphone and push-to-talk are used instead.

PROGRAMS
      arecord FILE.wav           record a microphone (arecord --level)
      radio_station /dev/mic F   transmit a microphone live on an SDR
      discord                    voice channels (see discord)
      audio.open_mic(side)       Python and Lua (see scripting)

    Settings > Sound picks the default speaker and microphone (written
    to /etc/sound.conf), their volume and gain, and shows a live level.
